# Roots

**Roots** is games in their time, and since: how each game was seen when it came out, what it came from, and how it's remembered. It's on to start (Settings > Features > **Roots: games in their time**); off, Squirrelcade is for collecting only, with no Roots section and nothing fetched for it.

## On a game's page

Every game's page has its **Roots** under your copy. First its place in its console's Top 100 and why it matters (where Squirrelcade has a text), then what its Wikipedia article says, read the first time you open the game (a few seconds) and kept:

- **A strip of dates:** its releases, the months its reviews came out, later reviews.
- **The aggregates:** Metacritic, GameRankings and OpenCritic, as its article's review table gives them. Metacritic's score for its console is also the number in the **Reviews** circle at the top of the page (IGDB's rating when Metacritic has none).
- **At the time**, its first 18 months (Settings > Roots), and the previews before:
  - **In the magazines:** each magazine that reviewed or covered it, with the issue and the page, and **Scan** when the article links the page's scan (the Internet Archive, the Video Game History Foundation).
  - **Reviews from the time:** each outlet's score and date, with the issue and page for a magazine.
  - **On the web, then:** web pages of its time, opened as they were through the Wayback Machine. A game from before 1995 says so: game websites began in 1995 and 1996.
  - **Videos of the day:** the videos its article cites; video channels of your own come later.
- **Where it came from:** the games its article names where it tells how the game was made (**What it came from**) and in its legacy (**What it led to**), each found in your consoles' catalogs, marked **Have it** or **Not yet**, and opening its own page. Magazines, people and words that aren't games are left out by not being in any catalog.
- **Looking back:** later reviews, writing since, its awards and best-selling place, and **Learn more**: its pages on Wikipedia and the reference sites (Settings > Roots).
- **Its series:** the series it's in (Mario, Super Mario...), a chip for each with how many of its games you have, and the chosen one's games as box art: the ones you have in color, this one outlined, each opening its page; **Open the series** goes to Nutshell › Series. It shows with Roots off too.

Some articles have two review tables, "Contemporary reception" and "Retrospective reception": Roots puts each where it says. A source with no date goes by its table. Nothing here is your own: it can all be read again.

## Its stars

A game's **stars** beside its name come partly from Roots: **#2 best-selling** on its console (Wikipedia's best-selling list for the console, where there is one) and its headline awards (Game of the Year, a BAFTA for Best Game, a Hall of Fame, from Wikidata). See [A game's page](collection.md#a-games-page).

## The Roots section

**Roots** in the menu has:

- **Consoles:** each console's history (with a few games to start with) and its **Top 100** list, by generation. Click a console's card for its page (its history first, else its Top 100), or its **History** or **Top 100** link. Each opens the console's page in Roots, with its **History** and **Top 100** tabs (see [Top 100 and history](top100-history.md)); its checklist stays in the Nutshell (Consoles), linked from there.
  A card shows:
  - **A picture of the console:** the photo at the top of its Wikipedia article. Squirrelcade fetches each one once, in the background, shrinks it and keeps it on your server (about 12 KB each), and looks again every three months. It is a Wikimedia Commons photo, never a company logo, and the console's own page credits it (**Picture: Wikimedia Commons**). A console whose article has no such photo, or with no internet, shows Squirrelcade's own console mark.
  - **Who made it and when:** "Sega · 1988–1997", or "Sony · 2020–" while it's on sale.
  - **Units sold,** short ("87.4 million sold"); hover for the full text. Nothing when it isn't known. Your own version of a console's history (its page's **Edit**) wins.
  - **Its Top 100:** the Stash's chest, a small bar and "41 of 93", opening the console's Top 100. A console you only follow shows it once you have any of its games.
- **Timeline:** year by year, the consoles that came out and the landmark games of each (the best of its Top 100 and its "Start here" games). Nutshell > Consoles has it too.

Both show the consoles you collect; **Consoles you don't collect too**, on Roots › Consoles, adds the others, and is remembered on that device (the timeline follows it).

### Top 20

**Roots > Top 20**: each year's twenty **Highest rated** games (IGDB's ratings, counting games rated by at least **Settings > Roots > Top 20 > Ratings a game needs for Highest rated**, 10) or **Best selling** (each year's chart from Wikipedia's "YYYY in video games": the United States to start, **Best selling: whose sales**; a year without that chart shows the one it has and says whose). Every year from this one back is a row: the year, then the chest with "you have 7 of 20" and a small bar, so you see your strong years without opening any; open one for its games as box art, each with its rank and the cover's banner. The newest year starts open, and a row of decades jumps down the page. A game counts as yours on any console it came out on. **Consoles you don't collect too** adds games only on consoles you don't collect. The ratings are worked out from what Squirrelcade already stores, and the charts are read once in the background (this year's again each week: System > Tasks > Read each year's best sellers), so the page only reads what's kept; the first visit after an IGDB update takes a moment.

### Consoles you follow

A console Roots knows that you don't collect (the Vectrex, the Game Boy, the Saturn, the arcade...) is **followed**: it gets what helps you know it, never what counts it. IGDB's list of its games (box art on its Top 100, details and ratings), your RomM's games for it (**In RomM**, **Play on RomM**, and its count on its card here), a page for each of its games (its Roots, videos, series and shops), and its games in the header's search and Store Mode, marked **Not tracked**. Never a checklist, a percentage, the Stash's numbers or acorns. Each card says **Followed** with **Stop following** (and **Follow** back); **Settings › Consoles › Consoles you follow** turns following off for all of them, for whoever minds the disk space (IGDB's list takes a few megabytes a console; the pictures aren't kept). A console becomes collected as before: own a few different games of it (Settings › Consoles › Unique games to track a console), or start collecting it now:

**Start collecting.** On a followed console's Roots page (and on its card, beside **Stop following**) **Start collecting** opens a short checklist before it does anything:

1. **Its list of games:** where its catalog will come from. A Wikipedia list where Squirrelcade knows one (the Vectrex, the Neo Geo Pocket Color and the Astrocade have one), else IGDB's list of its games, where each game counts once you own it. To use your own list, its Nutshell page takes a CSV once you start; for another Wikipedia page, use Settings › Catalogs and matching (Wikipedia lists to use).
2. **How your PriceCharting export names it:** the console names an import will put on this console ("Vectrex"). Where Squirrelcade doesn't know one, an import that names the console makes a console of its own.

Then **Start collecting** adds it to **Settings › Consoles › Consoles you collect before you own enough games**, starts its catalog in the background, and says where to go next: its page in the Nutshell, or Add a game. While you own none of its games, its Roots page says **Stop collecting**: the console is followed again, and the catalog made for it goes (your own list for it and your answers stay). Once you own enough games of it, it stays collected either way.

## Now in and the Time machine

**Roots > Now in** takes today's day and month a decade back at a time: on October 26, 2026, **Now in the 2010s** is October 26, 2016, then the 2000s, the 90s, the 80s and the 70s. Each shows the games that came out from two weeks before that day to two weeks after (the 70s two months either side, when there were few games and most were arcade ones), the most important first (IGDB's rating and how many ratings it rests on, a Top 100 place), each with its consoles' marks (yours in color), and its trailer to play right there. Under them, the magazines of yours in Leaves that were on sale then: a magazine's cover date ran a month or so ahead of the newsstand, so a November issue shows in October. Tap one to read it.

**Roots > Time machine** is the same for any day: pick it, or step a day, a week or a month at a time.

**On this day**, at the top of Now in, lists the games that came out on today's date in any year; Today's **This week in game history** has a **Month** side too: this month's most important games, every year. A game's timeline strip (on its page) shows its other releases from IGDB beside Wikipedia's: on later consoles, and its remakes and remasters. In Store Mode, a game's card has a line of its time: when it came out, a review of its day, and its review in your magazines.

The games come from IGDB's lists of your consoles and the ones you follow (Settings > Sources > IGDB), so a console Squirrelcade doesn't know has none. Settings > Roots > Now in and the Time machine: **Days either side of a date** (14), **A decade's own window** ("1970s: 60"), **Decades Now in goes back** (5) and **Games shown in a window** (20). Settings > Magazines: **How far a cover's date ran ahead** (a month) and **A magazine's own** ("Nintendo Power: 2").

## Videos

With **Settings › Roots › Videos on a game's page** on (it's off to start), a game's Roots plays its videos: **Videos of the day** under At the time (its trailers, commercials, the reviews of its first months), **Videos since** under Looking back. Tap one to play it right there, in YouTube's privacy-enhanced player (the whole screen on a phone; full screen from the player's own button), or **Watch on YouTube**. The thumbnails come from your server, which keeps a copy of each; YouTube's player loads only when you press play.

Where they come from:

- **Pasted by you:** under either list, paste a YouTube video's address, choose **Of its time**, **Since** or **By its date**, and **Add**. The × beside a video takes it off the game.
- **IGDB's trailers** for the game (with IGDB set up).
- **The videos its Wikipedia article cites**, playable when they're YouTube's.
- **Search YouTube for it**, at the bottom of every game's Videos tab: a plain YouTube search for the game and its console, opened in your browser. No key or setup, and nothing goes through your server.
- **Video channels** (Settings › Roots): IGN, GameSpot and Gaming Historian to start, others listed off (Game Informer, Nintendo Life, Push Square, Pure Xbox, Digital Foundry, Classic Game Room, Game Sack, Metal Jesus Rocks, LGR, My Life in Gaming, DidYouKnowGaming, GameTrailers). Each line is a name, the channel's @handle or address, and on or off; add "| archive" for a channel of old commercials or TV shows, whose videos count as their games' own time. **Read video channels** (System › Tasks) reads them at night and keeps each video whose title says what it is (a review, a trailer, a preview, a history, gameplay) and names one of your consoles' games. What it reads depends on **How much of YouTube to use** (below).

### Light or Deep

**Settings › Roots › How much of YouTube to use** has three choices:

- **Light** needs no key and no setup. Videos come from IGDB's trailers, the ones you paste, the ones Wikipedia cites, the **Search YouTube for it** link, and each video channel's **newest uploads**: Squirrelcade reads each channel's public feed at night (about its latest 15 videos) and keeps the ones about your games as it finds them, so a game's videos build up over time. It's **fewer videos**: a channel's older videos (IGN's review of a game from 2010, say) are only there with Deep. YouTube's public feeds come and go for some channels; one that doesn't answer is tried again the next night.
- **Deep** uses a YouTube key (below): each channel's whole list of videos, the date of a video you paste, and **Find its videos**.
- **Automatic** (the start) is Deep when a YouTube key is set and Light when none is, so adding a key upgrades it, and an install that already had a key keeps working as before.

With Light chosen, a key you've set isn't used. On System › Status the YouTube line says which one is working.

### A YouTube key

Free, about five minutes, in your Google account:

1. Go to **console.cloud.google.com** and sign in.
2. In the project picker at the top, **New project**, name it Squirrelcade, **Create**, and choose it.
3. **APIs & Services › Library**: search **YouTube Data API v3**, open it, **Enable**.
4. **APIs & Services › Credentials › Create credentials › API key**. Copy the key.
5. Click the key to edit it: under **API restrictions** choose **Restrict key**, tick **YouTube Data API v3**, **Save**.
6. In Squirrelcade, **Settings › Sources › YouTube (videos)**: paste it into **YouTube API key** and save.

With the key, each channel's whole list of videos is read once (a big channel like IGN's takes a few thousand of the day's 10,000 free units, and a while), then its newest videos each night; every 30 days the whole list is read again, as YouTube asks of what's kept from it, and a video gone from the channel goes from Squirrelcade too. Only the videos about your games are kept. A pasted video gets its date with the key too.

**Find its videos** (under a game's Videos of the day, in Deep) searches each video channel that's on for the game with YouTube's own search, and keeps what it finds like a channel's read. It finds the reviews a channel's list of uploads leaves out: YouTube lists only a channel's newest 20,000 videos, so IGN's and GameSpot's reviews from before about 2018 aren't in it. Each channel's search costs 100 of the 10,000 units a YouTube key gets a day; the button shows how many are left today, and Squirrelcade never goes past **YouTube's units Squirrelcade may use a day** (8,000, Settings > Roots), so the channels' nightly reading always has room. YouTube's day starts at midnight Pacific time.

## Settings > Roots

- **Read each game's Wikipedia article** (on): off, a game's Roots has only what comes with Squirrelcade, and Wikipedia isn't asked about games.
- **Pictures of the consoles** (on): the picture on each console's card, from its Wikipedia article, kept on your server. Off, or with no internet, the cards show Squirrelcade's own console marks, and nothing is fetched.
- **Look up the articles the catalogs don't link** (on): a game whose catalog doesn't link its article (one you added, or from your own list) is looked up on Wikipedia by its title, once.
- **At the time lasts** (18 months).
- **Videos on a game's page** (off), **Video channels**, and **Videos shown at first** (6): see [Videos](roots.md#videos).
- **Reference sites:** the sites **Learn more** links to, in order, each with the Wikidata properties that hold the game's identifier there ("MobyGames | P11688, P1933"), or a search address with {title}.

**Read games' Roots** (System > Tasks) reads the articles of the games you have and want at night, a few hundred at a time (Settings > Tasks), and an article read before only when Wikipedia changed it. It asks Wikipedia and Wikidata about one request a second, as they ask.
