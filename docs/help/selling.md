# Selling your games

Play a game, then sell it: Squirrelcade writes a copy's listing for eBay or Mercari from what it knows about it, tells you what it's worth and what a sale would leave you after fees and shipping, lists the photos to take, and keeps each sale, in money and in meals.

Nothing is posted for you. Squirrelcade has no eBay or Mercari account of yours: you read the listing over, copy it into the marketplace's form, and add the photos yourself.

## Ready to sell

Open a copy's **Ready to sell** from:

- its game's drawer, under Your copies (**ready to sell**);
- its window (**Change it, where it is, lending, photos...**), beside **Sold**;
- **Acorns > For sale**, beside a copy marked for sale;
- **Acorns > Sales**, from the games worth selling next (below).

It has a tab for **eBay** and one for **Mercari**, each with:

- **Title**: the game, its console and year, its condition in the words buyers search for ("CIB Complete", "Disc Only", "New Sealed"), its region when it's not yours ("PAL", "Japan Import"), and "Tested" when its last test or rip went well. At most 80 characters: the word "Tested" goes first, then the year, then the end of a long name.
- **Description**: what you get ("the disc, the case and the manual"), how it was tested and when, your note for buyers (the copy's For sale note), a line saying the photos show the copy you'll receive, and the end you give every listing (Settings > Collection > Selling: how you ship, returns). Mercari's is kept under its 1,000 characters.
- **Item specifics** (eBay): Platform, Game Name, Region Code, Release Year, Genre, Publisher (from IGDB when it's on), and the UPC when Squirrelcade knows the game's barcode (a scan in Store Mode, or another app's export).
- **Condition**: New for a sealed copy, For parts or not working when its last test or rip failed, Used otherwise (Mercari: New, Poor, Good).

Each has a **Copy** button. **Start a listing on eBay** (or Mercari) opens the marketplace's form in a new tab.

### The price

**PriceCharting's value** for its condition (from your last export), and **your asking price**: **Mark it for sale** saves it and puts the copy on Acorns > For sale. Under them, what a sale at that price would leave you: the marketplace's fee (eBay keeps 13.6% and 40 cents an order in the US, Mercari 10%) and what shipping costs you (5.00 by default) taken off. **What it sold for on eBay** opens eBay's sold listings for the game, the prices copies really went for; Mercari's tab has Mercari's.

### Photos

The copy's standard photos taken so far, and the ones still to take (for a disc game complete in its case: **Box front**, **Box back**, **Inside**, **Disc front** and **Disc back**). Take them in the copy's window (on a phone, each opens the camera; see [Your copies](your-copies.md#standard-photos)). **Download the photos** gives them in a zip, in that order and named by their place, to upload to the listing.

### Tested, and ripped

A buyer wants to know it works. In the copy's window, **Tested** records a test (**It works**, **Works, with problems** or **Doesn't work**), and the listing says when it was last tested and how it went.

On the consoles whose games you rip (Settings > Collection > Your copies > Consoles whose games you rip), the window also has **Ripped**: **Read fully**, **Read with errors** or **Couldn't be read**. A disc read in full is the best test there is, and the listing says "The disc was read in full and verified without errors on" that day. A rip shows as its own badge beside the copy's last test.

Ripping a disc means reading all of it into an image and checking it against [Redump](http://redump.org)'s list of verified dumps. On a PC, a Blu-ray drive flashed with [OmniDrive](https://github.com/RibShark/OmniDrive) does it for most consoles: an ASUS BW-16D1HT, or one of the LG drives OmniDrive lists. Use it with [redumper](https://github.com/superg/redumper), Redump's dumper, or MPF, its window. It reads CDs (PlayStation, Saturn, Sega CD, 3DO), DVDs (PS2, Xbox, Xbox 360, GameCube, Wii) and Blu-rays (PS3, PS4, PS5, Wii U, Xbox One, Series X), and those are the setting's consoles to start with. PSP's UMDs and Dreamcast's GD-ROMs need their own console. With a cartridge reader (the Open Source Cartridge Reader, say), add its consoles to the setting: the listing then says the cartridge was read in full.

## It sold

At the bottom of Ready to sell, **It sold**: what it sold for, where (eBay, Mercari, in person or elsewhere) and the day (today if you leave it). The fees are worked out unless you give them, and so is shipping (none for a sale in person). **Record the sale**: the copy leaves your collection as sold, and if PriceCharting still lists it, Stash updates > Send to PriceCharting reminds you to remove it there.

## Acorns > Sales

- **This month**, **this year** and **every sale**: what your sales left you after fees and shipping, how many, and how many **meals** that makes (a meal is 15.00 by default: Settings > Collection > Selling > What a meal costs). Every sale also says what they made over what the copies cost you (your price paid, else your estimate).
- **Ready to sell?**: the games worth selling next, the most valuable first. **Games you've finished** are the ones you marked beaten, completed or dropped (What you played); **Owned more than once** are the extra copies of games you have several of (the best copy stays: the most complete, then the most valuable). **Worth more lately** are the copies whose value rose the most since about a year ago (or since the first price Squirrelcade kept), the biggest rise first, with how much and since when: a good time to sell those. A copy already for sale isn't on them, nor one with your keeper tag ("Keeper" by default: Settings > Collection > Selling), so tag the games you'll never part with.
- **Sold**: each sale, with what it sold for, the fees and shipping, what it left you and what it made over its cost. **take back** undoes a sale that didn't go through: the copy comes back into your collection. **Download** gives every sale as a spreadsheet.

Your sales are yours: viewers never see them.

## Settings

Settings > Collection > Selling: eBay's fee and its fee per order, Mercari's fee (change them to your country's), what shipping a game costs you, what a meal costs, the keeper tag, the end of every listing, and your country's eBay (www.ebay.co.uk, www.ebay.de...) for its sold listings and its form.
