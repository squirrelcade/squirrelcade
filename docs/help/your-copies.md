# Your copies: places, tags, tests, photos, loans and selling

Beside what PriceCharting knows about a copy, Squirrelcade keeps what only you know: where it is, your own tags, when you tested it, photos of it, who you lent it to, and whether you'd sell or trade it. They stay through every collection update.

Each copy you own has its own details: your complete copy of a game and your loose copy of it, and two complete copies of the same game too (since 0.19.0).

## A copy's details

Open a game you own, and under **Your copies** click **Change it, where it is, lending, photos...** beside a copy:

- **The copy**: its condition, what you paid, the day you bought it and notes (see [Your collection](collection.md#adding-a-copy-in-squirrelcade)); **Sold** or **Remove** takes it out of your collection. Without a price paid, it has an **Estimated price** of your own, empty unless you fill it in (below).
- **Tested**: **It works**, **Works, with problems** or **Doesn't work**, with a note if you like ("the battery's dead"): each test is kept with its day and time, the newest first. Under Your copies, **tested: it works** records one in a tap. On the consoles whose games you rip, **Ripped** records how a full read of the disc went (see [Selling your games](selling.md#tested-and-ripped)).

- **Where it is**: a shelf, a box, a room... The places you've used before are offered as you type.
- **Your tags**: any words you like ("Favorite", "Signed", "Needs a case"). Up to 20 a copy.
- **Keeping it / For sale / For trade**, with an asking price and a note for buyers if you like.
- **Its digital copy**: for a disc that came with a digital license (Xbox's disc-to-digital, a code in the box): **Claimed** (with the day and the store), **Not claimed yet**, or **Not offered for this one**. A claimed license outlives the disc: sell the disc and the game still counts as owned, digitally, for Claude and AI apps ([Claude and AI apps](ai-apps.md)).
- **Photos**: the box, the cart, the condition. They're made smaller in your browser before they're sent (to 1600 pixels on their longest side by default, Settings > Collection > Photo size), and kept in Squirrelcade's database, so every backup has them. Up to 10 a copy (Settings > Collection > Photos per copy).

The drawer then shows each copy's details under it, and the Stash page shows them on its rows. Its filters narrow the list to a place, a tag, the games lent out, the ones for sale or trade, or the digital copies claimed (or not yet).

## Standard photos

Each copy has its standard photos, by what it has and what its console plays games from: a disc game complete in its box has **Box front**, **Box back**, **Inside**, **Disc front** and **Disc back**; a sealed copy only the box's front and back; a loose cartridge its **Cartridge front** and **Cartridge back** (a Switch, DS, 3DS or Vita game's **Card**). Each is a button in the copy's window: on a phone it opens the camera, and the photo takes that place (**retake** to replace it). Other photos go under **Add photos**, as before. Settings > Collection > Your copies names the standard photos (the box's sides, the manual, whatever you like; "{game}" becomes Disc, Cartridge, Card or Disk).

## Estimated prices

When you don't know what you paid for a copy (PriceCharting writes 0 for those), you can give it an **Estimated price** in its window, to get a sense of what you've spent. It stays empty unless you fill it in: Squirrelcade estimates nothing on its own. **Suggest** proposes one: a copy with its box at its console's usual price for a new game (and when the game came out, as when you most likely bought it); one without its box at half of that, most likely bought used. Keep it, change it, or clear it, then **Save the copy**.

An estimate is yours alone: it's never a price paid, never sent to PriceCharting or another service, and never in a download. The drawer shows it in grey ("estimated $49.99"), so does the Stash page's Paid column, and Statistics adds them up beside what you paid ("and $5,400 estimated by you for 108 without a price"). A copy with an estimate is off Stash > Improve's "price paid" list. Settings > Collection > Suggested estimates has each console's usual price (the defaults are US prices; change them to your currency), the share for a copy without its box, and a switch for the Suggest button.

## Improve your collection

**Stash > Improve** lists what your copies are missing: a price paid (or, when you don't know it, your estimate), the day bought, a standard photo, a test, where it's kept. Each has its tab with its count, and the console filter narrows it. **Fill in** opens the copy's window; **Next copy** goes on to the one after it, so you can work through them one at a time (a copy with nothing left leaves the list). Settings > Collection > Improve your collection chooses what counts; **Test a copy again after** (Your copies) brings a copy tested long ago back onto the list.

## Lending a game

In the same window, **Lend it to** a name, with the day it's due back (30 days from today by default: Settings > Collection > Lend for; empty for no day). **It's back** when it comes back.

**Stash > Loans** lists the games lent out (overdue ones first, in red) and the ones given back. With notifications set up (Settings > Notifications), you get one message when a lent game is overdue (**When a lent game is overdue**, on by default); changing its due day lets it remind you again.

## Selling and trading

**Ready to sell** (in a copy's window, the drawer, or beside a copy on For sale) writes its listing for eBay or Mercari, with its price after fees, its photos and, once it sells, the sale: see [Selling your games](selling.md).

**Acorns > For sale** lists the copies you marked, with their asking prices and values. **Download** gives the list as a spreadsheet, and **Share** makes a link anyone can open to see it without signing in (see [Sharing your collection](sharing.md)).

Under the list, **Owned more than once** shows the games you have several copies of, a place to find ones to sell: **sell or trade** opens a copy's details.

## Upgrades

**Stash > Upgrades** lists the games whose best copy isn't complete: loose, or without its box or its manual. A game you also have complete (or sealed, or graded) isn't on it. The ones you like best come first: your rating (in the game's drawer, under What you played), then IGDB's, then what your copy is worth. **Price on PriceCharting** opens the game's page there, with what a complete copy costs. Filter by what's missing, or to the games you rated. Viewers see it too, as gift ideas, without your ratings unless you share what you played.

## Viewers

Your copies' details are yours: viewers don't see them unless you turn on **Viewers see your copies' details** (Settings > Security > Viewers). Even then, they never change them.
