# Sets and series

## Sets

Your own sets of games beyond consoles: a publisher's releases (Limited Run Games, say), a series, any theme. Each has its completion, a filter by console, its missing and owned games, and a download of what's missing (with release numbers and values).

**Sets > New set**, then either:

- a **ready-made** set: **Limited Run Games** or **Super Rare Games** (one click fills in its Wikipedia page);
- a **Wikipedia list** page (its name or address): its tables of games are read, each game's consoles from a Platform column, from notes such as "(PS4)" in its row, or from the heading its table sits under ("Nintendo Switch"); or
- a **CSV** with Title and Platform columns (console names as PriceCharting writes them, or Squirrelcade's platform keys), and a release number column (No., Number or #) if the set numbers its games.

Whether you own a set's game is its console catalog's answer (your Review answers and mappings count). A game outside the console's catalog counts by its title, and an owned copy that only looks like it is pointed out ("the same game?"), for you to answer **Same game** or **Not it**, never counted on its own.

**Release numbers.** When the list numbers its releases (Super Rare Games' "Release No.", Limited Run's "Limited run #", or a No. column in your CSV), each game shows its number on its console, and the set opens on **All**: every game in number order, each console's own numbers together (Super Rare Games' Switch #1 to #163, then its Switch 2 #1 and #2), with **Have it** or **Need it**. A double pack listed as "#11 & #12" goes by its first number. A set made before numbers were read gets them with **Read again**.

**Value.** A game you have shows what your copies are worth (from your PriceCharting export or spreadsheet), and the summary adds them up. A game you don't have links to its prices on PriceCharting.

**Consoles.** A list can span consoles you don't collect for it (Super Rare Games makes Switch, Switch 2, PS4 and PS5 games). **Consoles** on a set's page chooses which count: tick Switch and Switch 2, and the PlayStation games leave the set, its percentage, Store Mode and the wishlist, while someone who collects for PlayStation ticks those instead. With none ticked, the set takes every console in its list, or only the consoles you have games for.

On a set's page you can add games by hand or take some out; both survive reading the list again. A missing game says which other consoles you have it on. Store Mode says which sets a game is in, and the wishlist can give a set's missing games acorns (Acorns > Acorns ranking > Region and ownership > "In one of your sets": 0 until you choose).

## Series

**Sets > Series** lists every series IGDB knows that you own a game of, with how many of its games (in your consoles' catalogs) you have, sorted by the most owned, the fewest missing or A to Z. The green part of each bar is what you own on a console; a blue part after it counts the games you don't have on a console but do have on PC (the PC library), with "+N on PC". Open a series for its games in release order, missing first or A to Z (your choice is remembered on that device). A remake or remaster stands right after the game it remakes, marked "remake of ..." (IGDB's links; they come with the next IGDB download). **Set its story order** (owners) puts a series in the order its story goes: move each game up or down (Yakuza 0 first); the series then opens in story order, each game's versions on different consoles together by release year, and a remake you didn't place takes its original's place. IGDB's series need IGDB keys.

**Set up** (owners) chooses which series show, with a check box for each, and adds series of your own: a name, and the words its games' titles have, separated by commas ("Yakuza" with "Yakuza, Like a Dragon"). Your own series come from your consoles' catalogs, show with the others marked **Yours**, and are listed whatever you own of them. Both are also settings (Settings > Collection > Series).
