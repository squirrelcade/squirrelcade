# The Acorns wishlist

The wishlist ranks the games you don't have, on the consoles you collect, by rules you set.

- **Acorns** say how much you'd want a game: Squirrelcade's own unit, so it's never taken for a review score. A game's acorns are the sum of what each rule gives it, and you can see every one: open a game (or its row) for the list.
- **Reviews** sit beside a game's acorns: IGDB's rating out of 100 (critics and players together) with a star, and how many ratings on hover. They show on the wishlist, a console's missing games (sortable), Coming soon, Past releases, Store Mode, Deals, Today and the game drawer.

## My wishlist or Suggestions

You choose what your wishlist is: **Acorns > Acorns ranking > Your wishlist > What your wishlist is**.

- **Suggestions** (the start, and what an install that already had a wishlist keeps): Squirrelcade's own ranked list, worked out from your catalogs and your rules. Games arrive on it by themselves.
- **Picked by you** (My wishlist): nothing is added for you. The Wishlist page has two tabs, **My wishlist** (the games you chose, ranked by their acorns) and **Suggestions** (the ranked list to go down). Press **Add** on a suggestion to put it on your wishlist; a game already there says "On your wishlist", with a way to take it off. **Take off** on My wishlist removes a game (it goes back to Suggestions).
- **Want it** appears on a game's page, beside its shops, when you don't have the game on that console, and in [Store Mode](store-mode.md) beside Add it. It adds the game to My wishlist; **On your wishlist** takes it off again.

A game you have on a console is never suggested for it, even when a catalog didn't match your copy: Squirrelcade also checks by title.

## Where the acorns come from

- **Reviews** from IGDB (with IGDB keys), trusted more when many people rated the game.
- **Your consoles and genres,** in the order you like them (the welcome guide starts them from your collection: what you collected lately, and the genres you own more of than their share).
- **Series you collect:** more acorns the more of a series you already have.
- **Completion:** acorns when a console is nearly complete.
- **Your preference** for a single game: Must Have, Strong Interest... down to **Do Not Recommend**, which keeps it off the list. Each level shows a mark (🔥 ⭐ 👍 👎 🚫) that follows its acorns. The picker is a small pill with the mark and the acorns it adds (🔥 +20); Settings > Interface > "Your preferences shown as" can show the name instead, or the mark alone.
- **Other acorns you can turn on:** being in one of your [sets](sets.md), being owned on PC (fewer acorns by default), another region's release, and more.

**Acorns > Acorns ranking** holds all of it. Each ranked list (consoles, genres) follows a curve you pick, with its entries dragged along it; everything else is a number with its description. Acorns are kept on a 0 to 100 scale: when your top ten land far from 90 to 100, every game's acorns are multiplied so the tenth best lands at 90, shown as a line of its own ("Fit acorns to the scale", a setting).

## Using the list

- **Snooze** a game for a few months to leave it off the list for now.
- Your **preference** for a game, from wherever a game you don't have is listed: the wishlist, the game's drawer, a console's page (its missing games), the Top 100 and History tabs, Roots > Timeline, sets, series, Coming soon, Past releases, Store Mode and Check a list. A change shows everywhere at once.
- **Download** gives the list as a spreadsheet.
- **Share** makes a link anyone can open without signing in, to see your top picks and where to buy them: for birthdays and holidays. See [Sharing your collection](sharing.md).
- **Share as a file** downloads your top picks as one web page to send by email or chat: each game's place, acorns, console and priority, with a small cover, dated and named for you (Settings > Friends > Your name in what you share). Nobody needs a link, an account or any access to your server: the page opens in any browser, works offline, has no prices paid or notes, and **Print / Save as PDF** in it makes a PDF. On the By platform tab, the file is that console's list. How many games it holds, and whether it has covers, are in Acorns ranking > Sharing. Between two Squirrelcades, [Friends](friends.md) already share the top of your wishlist in their files; the page is for everyone else.
- **Priority** (High, Medium, Low) comes from the acorns (the cut-offs are settings).

## The game of the day

**Settings > Notifications > Game of the day** (off until you turn it on): each day, one game you don't have from the top of your wishlist, at the top of the Wishlist page and, if you like, as a message at an hour you choose. It says why the game is there (where its acorns come from, its reviews, and why it matters when its console has a history), with the same stars beside its name and the same circles as a game's page, **Play on RomM** when your RomM has it (Settings > Sources > RomM links games you don't own), links to its price, and **Another one** draws again. The more acorns a game has, the likelier it's picked; a game that came out around this date in a past year gets a few more acorns (older games often cost less); a game isn't picked again for 30 days, nor another of its series for 5. The pick is Squirrelcade's own (no AI, no outside service), so it can't check a store's stock or price: the links do that.

## Deals

With a wishlist on PriceCharting, PriceCharting emails you when someone lists one of its games on eBay below the market value. With [collection updates from your email](collection.md#from-your-email) on, Squirrelcade reads those emails too (Acorns ranking > PriceCharting's deal emails > "Read PriceCharting's deal emails too", with **Accounts to read** for which email accounts). Nothing is fetched from PriceCharting or eBay: it's only what PriceCharting emails you.

**Acorns > Deals** lists the deals of the last 7 days:

- Each deal's price, how far below the market value it is, whether you already have the game, and its place on your wishlist here, with links to the listing and to PriceCharting.
- The best deals first (the biggest saving against the market value), or your wishlist's games first. Pick one console, or only new or sealed copies (as the seller's listing title says: PriceCharting's emails don't give the condition).
- Today shows the top three the same way, and the game of the day gives a game with a fresh deal a few more acorns.

PriceCharting emails every deal on its wishlist. To be told only about the ones that matter, **Acorns ranking > PriceCharting's deal emails > "Tell me about deals on my wishlist's top"** (25, say) sends a message only for the deals on games your Squirrelcade wishlist ranks that high.

Prices for new copies in shops aren't read anywhere (no shop or price site offers them to apps like this). The shop links in each game's drawer (Deku Deals for Switch games, Amazon, Best Buy...) open a search there.

## Coming soon and Past releases

- **Acorns > Coming soon:** the games not out yet on the consoles you collect, soonest first, with whether you already have a copy and its acorns. **Calendar file** downloads the dated releases for Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar. Release reminders can send a message on the day ([Notifications](notifications.md)).
- **Acorns > Past releases:** the games that came out around this time of year in each of the last few years (four by default; Settings > Interface), since a game a year or more old often costs less than at release, with links to their prices.

## The PC wishlist

With the [PC library](pc-library.md) on, **Acorns > PC wishlist** lists PC games worth buying, found through IGDB (the genres you like, the series you collect, and the PC versions of console games you keep sealed), ranked with Steam's review scores. Everything you own on PC is left out. With **PC game prices** on (Settings > Features, and a free IsThereAnyDeal key), it shows each game's best price now, how much off it is, and its lowest price ever, and can tell you when one drops (see [PC library](pc-library.md)).

## Shop links

Each game's drawer links to PriceCharting and to the shops you choose (Settings > Interface > Shop links: a name and the shop's search address, with `{title}` and `{platform}` in it, and optionally the consoles it's for). A fourth part names its group. To start with there are 18, in groups: used marketplaces (eBay, Mercari, FB Marketplace, OfferUp), game stores (eStarland, DKOldies for older consoles, GameStop), new games (Amazon, Best Buy, Walmart, Target for current consoles, Deku Deals for Switch), imports and PAL (Play-Asia, Solaris Japan), limited releases (Limited Run Games, Super Rare Games) and off the beaten path (Whatnot, ZenMarket for Japanese auctions); up to 40 in all. In a game's drawer they fold under **Shop for it**; lists show the first three. Squirrelcade doesn't contact the shops: the links open their search in your browser.
